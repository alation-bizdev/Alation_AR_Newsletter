A Note from the CEO · Q2 2026
Satyen Sangani CEO & Co-Founder, Alation

I've spent the past several weeks with customers at some of the industry's most important gatherings, and two conversations in particular stayed with me.

At Snowflake Summit, I joined Lutz Beck, CIO of Daimler Truck North America, to discuss what it takes to go from pilots to production. At DTNA, agents help material planners navigate supplier, inventory, and warehouse systems — removing operational friction so people can focus on higher-value work.

At the Gartner Data & Analytics Summit, I sat down with Robin Rietveldt, Chief Data & Analytics Officer at Lipton Teas and Infusions, to talk about unlocking AI with metadata and governance. The insight that stuck: Don't build foundations in the abstract. Build the foundation required to make one important use case work. Then scale from proof, not theory.

The pattern I keep hearing across these conversations: start with the business process, ground it in trusted data, and measure value relentlessly.

We have some exciting news to share on July 1. Stay tuned.

Georgia-Pacific · 32,000 employees · 150+ facilities

$25M in Savings from a Trusted Data Foundation

Georgia-Pacific was repurchasing spare parts across sites because teams couldn't trust the data showing what was already in inventory. Thirty data sources held the answer, but inconsistent numbers had eroded confidence to the point where people stopped checking.

"When we get into your data solution, I look at the numbers one day, and then the next day they double — it's all over the place. I can't trust it."
— Matt Robuck, VP of Data, Georgia-Pacific

Business Impact

  • $25 million in intercompany parts transfers last year, avoiding external purchases
  • Deployed 11 AI agents in 2025 and is building more
  • Governance and trust signals embedded directly into AI and operational workflows

As Matt Robuck noted, governance has become foundational to ensuring AI systems are trusted and usable across the enterprise.
